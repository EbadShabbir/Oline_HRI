CLARA: A Cascaded Language Architecture for Offline Personalized Robotic Assistance

Abstract

Personalized conversational robots must balance useful recall, response quality, and limited onboard computing resources. We present CLARA, a Cascaded Language Architecture for Robotic Assistance, designed to support offline personalized dialogue on a Jetson Orin Nano with 8 GB of memory. CLARA combines small and larger language models with separate decisions about model size and personal-memory retrieval. Its memory pipeline integrates lexical and semantic retrieval with persistent storage, seven-day retention, correction and deletion, and evidence-based response validation. Serialized model loading bounds concurrent model residency, while validation checks withhold responses that violate grounding requirements. We compare seven model-pair configurations and evaluate the selected 0.6B–1.7B cascade through a fictional seven-day development benchmark and process-restart recall tests. On the 30-case benchmark, the system delivers 22 application-valid responses and withholds eight; these structural outcomes do not establish semantic correctness. Median text-pipeline latency is 2.67 seconds, with a 95th percentile of 54.16 seconds. Persistent storage survives process restart, but only three of seven known-fact queries are answered correctly. These results expose a gap between functioning memory infrastructure and reliable personalized generation. CLARA provides an implemented architecture and empirical analysis of the tradeoffs among model residency, response latency, evidence validation, and personalization on constrained robot hardware.
